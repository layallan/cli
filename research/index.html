Research

We develop Light Intelligence for scientific discovery and new applications. Our research ranges from probing cells and tracking energy in molecules to connecting quantum devices, automating experiments, and shaping electron beams for X-ray sources.

Light Intelligence

We use inverse design to choose how light interacts with a sample. Its measured response helps us test a physical model and decide what to probe next.

Physics-guided learningInverse designProgrammable optics

Biosensing, Precision Health and Biopharmaceutical Discovery

Intelligent imaging, molecular sensing, and multimodal inference for personalized medicine, biological characterization, and therapeutic development.

Intelligent imagingMolecular sensingMultimodal inferenceTherapeutic discovery

Light for Energy and Climate

We use timed pairs of laser pulses to excite a sample and measure its response. Following how energy moves after excitation helps us study the molecular processes behind energy conversion and catalysis.

Ultrafast pump–probe measurementPhysics-guided inferenceCatalysis and energy conversionSustainable chemistry

Interconnects for Quantum Computing

A quantum device may emit light at a different wavelength from the one another device can absorb. We study how four-wave mixing in gas-filled capillaries can bridge that mismatch while preserving the phase of the optical signal.

Quantum transductionPhase-coherent four-wave mixingPhotonic interconnects

Autonomous Science and Advanced Instruments

Physics-grounded digital twins, adaptive experiments, and intelligent control for lasers, accelerators, advanced light sources, and scientific instrumentation.

Physics-grounded digital twinsAdaptive experimentsIntelligent instrument controlLasers, accelerators, and light sources

High-Brightness Electron Beams and X-ray Sources

At a photocathode, the timing of the laser pulse helps set when electrons are emitted. We shape that pulse and measure how charge and energy are distributed along the accelerated bunch. We also study how shaping the laser pulse in a Compton scattering source changes the X-ray spectrum.

Photocathode laser shapingLongitudinal phase spaceFree-electron lasersLaser–Compton X-ray sources
